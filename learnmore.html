Explore our seed varieties, discover ways to use familiar herbs, and learn the basics of growing plants at home. Whether you are new to gardening or have years of experience, every garden begins with a seed.

Discover Our Plants and Seeds

Every plant has its own characteristics and growing needs. Use these introductions to learn more about the varieties available at Celso's Semillas.

Papalo Seeds

Papalo is an aromatic herb used in Mexican and other Latin American cuisines. Its fresh leaves have a distinctive, bold flavor and can be enjoyed with tacos, sandwiches, salsas, and other dishes.

Growing tip: Give papalo warm weather, plenty of sunlight, and well-draining soil. Allow the soil to drain between waterings.

Harvest: Pick young, healthy leaves as the plant grows.

Epazote Seeds

Epazote is a traditional Mexican culinary herb with a strong, distinctive aroma. It is used in some bean dishes, soups, and regional recipes.

Growing tip: Epazote generally grows well in warm conditions and sunny locations with well-draining soil.

Harvest: Harvest small amounts of leaves as needed. Use this herb in moderation, since its flavor is strong.

Chile Pequin Seeds

Chile pequin produces small, spicy peppers that are popular in Mexican cooking. The fruits can add considerable heat to sauces, salsas, and seasonings.

Growing tip: Provide warmth, sunlight, regular watering, and soil with good drainage. Peppers need a sufficiently long warm growing season.

Harvest: Pick peppers when they reach the desired stage of maturity, using care when handling hot fruit.

Arugula Seeds

Arugula is a leafy green known for its peppery taste. It works well in salads, sandwiches, and as a fresh finishing green for many meals.

Growing tip: Arugula generally grows best in cool weather. Give it fertile soil and consistent moisture. Hot weather can make leaves more bitter and encourage flowering.

Harvest: Pick young leaves for a tender texture and milder flavor.

Sweet Basil Seeds

Sweet basil is a fragrant culinary herb often used in pasta, salads, sauces, and homemade pesto.

Growing tip: Basil prefers warm weather, sunlight, and well-draining soil. Water near the soil rather than continually wetting the leaves.

Harvest: Pinch growing tips to encourage a fuller plant, and remove flower buds when you want to encourage leafy growth.

Holly Berry Seeds

Holly plants are commonly grown for their foliage and colorful berries. Their appearance, growth habit, and berry production depend on the particular holly species and variety.

Growing tip: Check the exact variety for its sunlight, soil, and climate requirements. Some hollies need a compatible male plant nearby to produce berries.

Important: Holly berries can be poisonous to people and pets. Do not eat them.

Dandelion Seeds

Dandelions are hardy flowering plants known for their bright yellow flowers and familiar seed heads. They can support some pollinators and grow readily in many settings.

Growing tip: Dandelions adapt to many soil conditions and can grow in full sun or partial shade.

Garden note: They spread easily by seed, so consider where you plant them and whether you want them to spread.

Mohawk Sacred Tobacco Seeds

Tobacco is a plant with a long cultural history. The name on a seed packet alone may not establish the exact species, variety, or cultural origin.

Growing tip: Check the seed packet for the botanical name and variety before planting. Tobacco plants generally need warmth and a suitable growing season.

Important: Tobacco contains nicotine, which is addictive and toxic. This plant is not safe to consume or use casually.

Sunflower Seeds

Sunflowers are cheerful flowering plants that can bring color to a garden. Their flowers can attract pollinators, and some varieties produce seeds enjoyed by birds and people.

Growing tip: Choose a sunny spot and soil with good drainage. Tall varieties may need extra space and protection from strong winds.

Harvest: For seed collection, allow the flower head to mature and dry before collecting seeds.

Pink Crape Myrtle Seeds

Crape myrtle is a flowering ornamental shrub or small tree known for its summer blooms. Pink-flowering varieties can add long-lasting color to landscapes.

Growing tip: Crape myrtles generally prefer full sun and well-draining soil. Allow enough room for the mature size of the variety.

Patience: Seed-grown plants can vary in their characteristics, and flowering may take time. Confirm the variety's growing needs before planting.

A note about seed varieties: Growing times, mature size, germination rates, and care requirements vary by species, variety, and local conditions. Check the instructions provided with each seed packet for the most specific guidance.

Gardening Basics for Beginners

You do not need a large farm or an elaborate garden to begin growing plants. A sunny garden bed or suitable container can be a great place to start.

Five Tips for Growing from Seed

  1. Choose the right location. Learn how much sunlight your plant needs before deciding where to grow it.
  2. Prepare the soil. Use a suitable seed-starting mix for containers, or improve garden soil as needed. Containers should have drainage holes.
  3. Plant at the correct depth. Follow the seed packet instructions. Some seeds need light to germinate and should not be buried deeply.
  4. Water carefully. Keep the growing medium appropriately moist without leaving seeds or roots sitting in water.
  5. Be patient and observe. Germination times differ. Watch for new growth, adjust watering as needed, and protect young plants from extreme weather.
